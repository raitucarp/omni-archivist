"The syndicates will not send money for fuel now," Drolma observed, leaning her forearms against the frosted railing and looking down into the cloud-filled gulf. "The Ledger Syndicate won't pay the maintenance contracts for the turbines. They won't send helicopters with rations. Down in the valleys, the state boards will be screaming for someone to arrest."

"Let them scream," Bamidele said, their adire scarf catching the alpine breeze like an indigo pennant against the snow. "We have the hydro plant. We have the thermal taps. And before the week is out, every university from Tashkent to Trinidad will know that this mountain is where mathematics became whole."

A choice hung before them in the sharp alpine air, clear and unburdened.

They could slip down the southern passes before the corporate authorities in the capital organized their reprisals. They could take their families, scatter into the high, unmapped villages of the Gorno-Badakhshan valleys, and disappear into the agrarian rhythms of their ancestors, leaving the historic crucible of the revolution to be scavenged by corporate bailiffs or reclaimed by winter ice. It was the safe path, the instinct of the fugitive.

Or they could stay.

They could remain here on the roof of the world, keeping the turbines turning and the quartz consoles clean. They could transform this fortified redoubt—once an instrument of corporate opacity—into an unfortified, borderless sanctuary for every thinker, every student, and every scholar on Earth who wished to study the living syntax of numbers without paying tribute to an imperium.

Gulchehra drew the cold air into her chest, feeling the steady, unbroken beat of her heart against her ribs. She looked at Drolma’s weathered, laughing face; she looked at Bamidele’s bright, defiant eyes; and then she turned to face the vast, sun-drenched expanse of the Pamir peaks.

"These mountains were never theirs to own," Gulchehra said, her voice clear and resonant, carrying out over the silent glacial valleys below. "The redoubt belongs to the minds who defended it. We do not run. We stay here, we open the gates, and we teach the world how to count again."
