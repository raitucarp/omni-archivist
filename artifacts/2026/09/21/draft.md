

Across the cold perimeter of the Gordimer sector, where interstellar vacuum was once measured in pristine microkelvins, the Taurus Molecular Cloud was bleeding out.

Dr. Renata Vane stood within the forward observation bubble of the primary sorting spine, her magnetic boots locked to the vibration-deadened deck plates. Before her curved quartz-spinel viewport stretched three-dimensional non-Euclidean industrial scaffolding—thousands of kilometers of carbon-nanotube trusses, extraction umbilicals, and campanulate collector bells that flared open like carnivorous flowers against the star-strewn dark. The harvest apparatus did not merely gather matter; it devoured it. It was the bottom of the inning for pristine interstellar volatiles, an acknowledged exhaustion that Consortium executives in distant core worlds refused to transcribe into their actuarial forecasts.

Centuries ago on Old Earth, the catastrophic 1600 eruption of Huaynaputina had cast an aerosol veil that triggered global volcanic winters, demonstrating the sudden fragility of a closed planetary biosphere. Here, humanity had turned that mechanism inside out. By strip-mining the primal nurseries of space, they were enforcing an irreversible thermodynamic change upon the cosmological continuum itself.

"Intake array forty-six through ninety calibrated to local gradient," Renata spoke into the suit’s bone-conduction microphone, her voice flat, clipped, and clinical. Silver-grey eyes tracked the cascading spectral telemetry on her retinally coupled display. "Radiation pressure holding at zero point eight micro-dynes per square centimeter. Beginning boundary layer survey of dense core Barnard seven."

Beside her console, the digital telemetry streams flickered with faint, digitized radio-thermal hiss. The mission seemed straightforward on paper: calibrate the intake vanes of the campanulate sieves, balance them against stellar wind vectors, and map the boundary layer for the next quarter section of harvesting space. Every quadrant was cataloged meticulously under a rigorous compound number, stripping the sublime majesty of cosmic gestation down to cold accounting metrics. Yet beneath the procedural sterility, an acrid knot of dread sat in Renata’s gut.

She had designed these intake manifolds.

A decade ago, her post-doctoral equations on non-equilibrium fluid dynamics in high vacuum had been heralded as a triumph of applied astrochemical engineering. She had conceived the catalytic throats that could track and siphon trace volatiles from tenuous nebular fringes with unprecedented efficiency. She had marketed them as minor surgery upon comet halos, an innocuous skimming of molecular excess. The Consortium had taken those elegant proofs and scaled them into an insatiable strip-mining engine.

"Renata, watch your secondary intake manifold on the port flank," came the dry, gravelly transmission from Dr. Osei Calderon, monitoring from the lower observatory. "The Argentine spectroscopic module is detecting uncharacterized drift vectors southeast by east. Local density gradients are behaving erratically."

"Acknowledging, Osei," she replied, fingers dancing over tactile membrane keys. "Adjusting magnetic baffles to compensate."

Far below the station’s trussed spine, the massive gas dwarf that anchored this extraction node loomed like a bruised eye, its upper stratosphere churned by an enormous atmospheric depression. Across its turbulent sheath, fluctuating barometric pressure sent rhythmic gravitational perturbations rippling through the harvesting array. The orbital tenders—weather-beaten workhorses bearing the weathered stencils of the Chippewa and St. John deep-field survey fleets—patrolled the ridged surface of floating asteroidal slag heaps like mechanical scavengers.

A red diagnostic warning strobed across her visor.

*Alert: Ambient hydride abundance sub-threshold.* 

Renata leaned forward, breath catching against the polycarbonate collar of her environmental suit. She re-initialized the sensors, forcing the sub-millimeter array to listen for the canonical 4.3-micron emission lines of raw ammonia and carbon chains. The spectrum was bone-white. Where the pristine core should have sung with molecular hydrides, there was only empty, exhausted baseline. Even the basic NH radical species, the indispensable chemical bricks without which stellar genesis choked in its cradle, registered at fractions of a percent of historical values.

Then the slag cloud hit.

A abrasive wall of particulate back-scatter, sheared from the tailings of nearby asteroidal strip-mines by shifting radiation pressure, slammed into the collector array. Hull plates shrieked through conductive resonance. Micro-meteoritic dust, sharp as powdered obsidian, scoured the outer intake cowlings, lacerating the delicate polymer layers of the sensor skin like an acute diverticulitis perforating vital reactor plumbing. 

"Pressure spike in the secondary throat!" Renata said, her pulse drumming against her ears. "Vanes forty-eight and forty-nine have jammed!"

Through the viewport, she saw the campanulate bell shudder. Unrefined tholin clathrates, accreting prematurely against the freezing intake throats, had formed a jagged crystalline crust. The mechanical joints fought against the icy weld, drawing excessive current until the warning strobes flared actinic violet. "Diverting tertiary cryo-coolant to blowback vents," she murmured, her hands moving with icy precision despite the cold sweat prickling her brow. "Purging manifold forty-eight."

Humanity had not merely harvested the cloud; they had murdered it before it could be born.

Every civilization world humming with power, every planetary habitat glowing with artificial warmth across the Orion Spur, had purchased its illumination by devouring the dawn of future worlds. Renata looked down at the data module glowing amber beneath her fingers.
